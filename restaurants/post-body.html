McDonald’s takes in more of Americans’ restaurant money than any other chain. It is not on Forbes’ first list of America’s Favorite Restaurants, published Nov. 17. Neither are Starbucks, Taco Bell, Dunkin’, Wendy’s, Burger King, Domino’s or Subway. Of the 10 chains where Americans spent the most in 2025, according to the market research firm Circana, only Chick-fil-A, at No. 5, and Chipotle, at No. 88, made the top 100.

The list ranks chains by what their customers say, not by sales. Forbes and the research firm HundredX collected 552,000 pieces of feedback from more than 100,000 people between October 2025 and September 2026, asking them to rate chains they had visited on food quality, menu, atmosphere and cleanliness, speed and price. A chain needed at least 300 full reviews to qualify, and those with a national footprint got a small boost.

Culver’s, the Wisconsin chain known for ButterBurgers and frozen custard, ranked first. In-N-Out Burger was second, HTeaO, a Fort Worth iced-tea chain founded in 2009, third, and Texas Roadhouse fourth. The 100 run from burger stands to steakhouses: 57 are counter-service chains and 43 are sit-down restaurants.

Darden Restaurants, the Orlando company behind Olive Garden, owns nine of the 100: LongHorn Steakhouse, Seasons 52, Cheddar’s Scratch Kitchen, Chuy’s, The Capital Grille, Yard House, Ruth’s Chris Steak House, Eddie V’s and Olive Garden itself. Eight of them list Orlando as headquarters, which is why Florida, with 16, is home to nearly as many of the chains as Texas, with 17. California has 14 and Georgia 10.

What customers rate highest

Forbes lists the areas where each chain scored best. Counter-service chains won on taste and speed. Sit-down chains won on atmosphere and menu. Only two of the 57 counter-service chains scored best on atmosphere, and only one sit-down chain on speed.

Price rarely came first for anyone. Three chains led on it: Hungry Howie’s Pizza, Pollo Tropical and Waffle House.
