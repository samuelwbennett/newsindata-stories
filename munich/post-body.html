On Sunday morning the Detroit Lions play a home game 4,300 miles from Detroit. They face the New England Patriots at Allianz Arena in Munich at 9:30 a.m. ET on Fox. It's the first time the Lions have given up a game at Ford Field to play abroad, and the record suggests the "home" label won't be worth much.

Before you scroll, make a guess.

Since the NFL began playing regular-season games outside the U.S. in 2005, the designated home team has gone 29-39-1. That's a 43% win rate. In their own stadiums over roughly the same years, from 2005 through 2025, NFL home teams won 56% of the time.

Home in name only

In a game abroad, neither team sleeps in its own bed. Both fly overseas and cross several time zones, and the crowd is often split, with plenty of fans wearing jerseys from teams that aren't playing. The home team keeps its name on the schedule, its uniforms and its share of the gate, but it loses the things researchers usually credit for home-field advantage: familiar surroundings, no travel and a crowd on its side.

On average the designated home team has lost these games by 3.1 points. At home stadiums from 2005 through 2025, home teams won by 2.2 points on average, so the swing is about five points a game.

Some of the gap comes from who gets picked. Teams that agree to give up a home game have often been teams with smaller crowds or weaker records, so the designated home team has frequently been the worse team. This analysis doesn't adjust for team strength or betting lines, so it can't say how much of the 13-point gap in win rate is the trip and how much is the matchups.

London, Toronto and the Jaguars

London has hosted 45 of the 69 games, and home teams there are 18-26-1. The Bills played six home games in Toronto from 2008 to 2013 and won one.

The Jacksonville Jaguars have played abroad more than any other team, 16 times, and they were the home team in 14 of those games. They are 7-7 in that role. Take the Jaguars out and the rest of the league's designated home teams are 22-32-1.

This season has been the worst yet for home teams abroad. In seven games in Melbourne, Rio de Janeiro, London, Paris and Madrid, they are 1-6 and have lost by an average of 9.1 points. The one win was Jacksonville's 19-17 victory over the Eagles in London in October.

Germany has been the exception

Germany is the one place where home teams have done well. They are 4-1 in Munich, Frankfurt and Berlin, winning by an average of 3.4 points. Both earlier games at Allianz Arena went to the home team: the Buccaneers over the Seahawks in 2022 and the Panthers over the Giants in overtime in 2024.

The only home team to lose in Germany was New England. The Patriots were the designated home team in Frankfurt in 2023 and lost to the Colts 10-6. On Sunday they're the visitors, and the visiting role has suited them: they're 3-0 abroad as the road team, including a 45-7 win over the Rams in London in 2012, the most lopsided loss for any home team abroad. The Lions are 1-1 abroad, both times as the road team in London.

What 69 games can't settle

Sixty-nine games is a small sample, about a quarter of one regular season. A few close finishes going the other way would move the win rate several points, and Germany's 4-1 record rests on five games. The direction has held across eras, though. Home teams abroad won 38% of games from 2005 to 2019 and 45% from 2022 on.

Nor does the trip seem to hurt teams afterward. An ESPN analysis of London games from 2007 to 2015 found that the teams involved went 124-124-2 over the rest of those seasons.
