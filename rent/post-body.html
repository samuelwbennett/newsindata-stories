In the Miami metro area, 66.8% of renter households spent at least 30% of their income on rent and utilities in 2025, according to new Census Bureau estimates. That's the highest share of any metro area with at least 100,000 renter households. It's also well above New York at 52.7% and San Francisco at 46.8%, even though San Francisco's median rent is $329 a month higher than Miami's.

Nationally, 51.8% of renters, about 22.5 million households, crossed that 30% line, the standard the federal government uses to call a household cost-burdened. About 11.4 million, or 26.2%, spent half or more of their income on rent. Both shares are about where they were in 2021, when 51.1% were cost-burdened.

Rent alone doesn't decide the burden

San Jose has the highest median rent of any metro area, $2,889 a month, yet only 44.3% of its renters are cost-burdened, the lowest share among the 42 metros with at least 200,000 renter households. The reason is income: the median renter household there earns $123,403 a year, nearly twice Miami's $63,543. In Miami, a year of median rent equals about 41% of the median renter's income; in San Jose, about 28%.

Across the 71 metros with at least 100,000 renter households, rent levels track the burden only loosely. What predicts it is rent relative to what renters earn, and that ratio is highest in Miami and in inland California metros like Fresno, Bakersfield and Riverside. Riverside-San Bernardino, at 60.7%, and Orlando, at 59.8%, follow Miami among large metros.

Florida stands out

Florida has the highest share of cost-burdened renters of any state, 61.3%, followed by Connecticut at 55.6% and California at 55.5%. North Dakota has the lowest, 34.7%, the only state where fewer than four in 10 renters cross the line.

Eight of the 20 metro areas with the highest shares are in Florida, including Sebring, Gainesville, Miami, Deltona-Daytona Beach and Port St. Lucie, each above 63%. The highest share of all 392 metros is in Vineland, N.J., at 71.6%. The lowest are in the upper Midwest and Plains: Fond du Lac, Wis., at 27.6% and Bismarck, N.D., at 29.7%.
