Updated 12:25 p.m. Eastern on Nov. 17, 2026 · NSA Net was working only intermittently at last report, and the agency has not commented

The National Security Agency's classified internal network, NSA Net, went down on Tuesday, Nov. 17, CNN reported, citing six people familiar with the matter. It is the sixth major technology failure in the United States in six days, after outages at Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration, Workday, JPMorgan Chase and United Airlines, and none of the six has been blamed on a cyberattack.

As of 12:25 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, no outlet had reported NSA Net fully restored. A person familiar with it told Recorded Future News the network was providing intermittent service rather than none, and that arrivals at Fort Meade were staggered so tens of thousands of employees would not all sign in at once. The agency had not commented, and CNN's sources said there was no evidence of a cyberattack.

What NSA Net is

NSA Net is the network the agency's employees use to share classified information with one another. While the agency works out how far the problem reaches, it has staggered when employees arrive for work, according to CNN's sources. Another person told Recorded Future News that the network had been unstable for weeks. A current and a former U.S. official told CNN the outage would not stop urgent intelligence from reaching President Trump, who is traveling in Asia, because the government has several backup networks for that.

Six days of failures

The run began on Thursday, Nov. 12, when Microsoft 365 failed at about 11:17 a.m. Eastern and took Outlook, Teams and Copilot down in more than 70 countries. Microsoft's preliminary report puts the outage at nearly 24 hours, and the company blamed a software defect exposed by network work.

The next afternoon the FAA stopped every departure in the country for 4 hours and 21 minutes, only the third nationwide ground stop in its history, after a telecommunications failure at its air traffic control centers. About an hour after that stop began, Workday, which runs human resources and payroll for about 11,500 employers, went down during open enrollment season and was back by Saturday.

Monday brought two more. Chase customers reported trouble from the morning until about 6:30 p.m. Eastern, which the bank blamed on a software bug in one of its own hardware systems, and United delayed departures for about an hour and 40 minutes because pilots could not get the weight calculations they need before takeoff.

Laid end to end, the four finished outages with reported times add up to roughly 35 to 40 hours, about a day and a half of downtime at major American systems in less than a week. The range comes from Chase, whose problems began sometime Monday morning, and the total leaves out Workday, which has not said when service returned.

What is not known

What caused the outage, when it began, how many of the agency's employees and sites are affected and when the network will return. The NSA has not said whether it is related to any of the week's other failures, and no official or major outlet has reported a link.
