Big School Districts Have Voted To Close 193 Schools In The Past Year As Enrollment Falls

Nineteen large school districts have voted since November 2025 to close or consolidate 193 schools, according to a tracker kept by the education trade publication K-12 Dive. Cleveland is closing 29, Tulsa 17 and Philadelphia 17 over the next decade. Nearly all of them point to the same cause: fewer children.

Districts are paid largely by the student, and half-empty buildings still need heat, staff and repairs. Tulsa’s enrollment fell about a fifth over the past decade, to 32,500, and its board voted Oct. 19 to close 14 elementary schools, two middle schools and an alternative program, expecting to save $11.1 million. In St. Louis, where enrollment has fallen 58.5% since 1991, the board voted 4-3 on Nov. 10 to close nine of its 61 schools.

Closures nationwide

Across the country, 1,064 public schools closed before the 2024-25 school year, the latest federal count, up from 849 before 2021-22. That is the third straight annual increase. It is not a record: more than 2,000 schools a year closed after the Great Recession, and 1,230 closed before 2019-20.

The federal count includes charter, special education and alternative schools, which open and close more often than neighborhood schools. Schools of the regular type made up 836 of the 1,064. The count also lags: most of the closures districts approved this year won’t show up in federal data until 2027 or later.

Fewer babies, then fewer students

The United States recorded 4,316,233 births in 2007, the most in its history. Those children started kindergarten in 2012 and graduated from high school in 2025. Births have fallen most years since, to 3,606,400 in 2025, 16% fewer, according to provisional data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Each class entering school now is smaller than the one leaving.

Public schools enrolled 50.8 million students in the fall of 2019. The pandemic pushed that down by 1.4 million in a year as families turned to home schooling, private schools and later starts, and the National Center for Education Statistics projects enrollment will keep falling to 46.9 million in 2031, about 3.9 million below 2019. That projection was made before the recent drop in immigration. New York City’s public schools lost about 23,000 students this fall, according to Chalkbeat, a decline news reports have tied to housing costs and immigration enforcement.

Where schools are closing

Over the last two years of federal data, Minnesota closed schools at the highest rate, about 49 for every 1,000, followed by South Dakota, Ohio and Florida. Texas closed the most, 306. Hawaii, which runs a single statewide district, closed none.

More votes are coming. Broward County’s superintendent recommended on Nov. 5 closing 10 more schools for 2027-28, Indianapolis proposed closing six on Nov. 17, and Portland, Ore., is weighing scenarios that would close up to 14. Not every plan survives: in October, Minneapolis dropped a proposal that could have closed up to 16 schools after families objected.
