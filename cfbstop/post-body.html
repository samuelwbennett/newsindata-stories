When the FAA stopped every departure in the country at 3:54 p.m. Eastern on Friday, college football teams across the country were getting ready to fly to Saturday's games. By Saturday morning, nine of the 56 FBS games on the schedule had new kickoff times. Eight moved later. One moved earlier. And No. 19 Ole Miss at Oklahoma won't be played Saturday at all. It kicks off Sunday at 1 p.m. ET, 20 hours and 45 minutes after it was meant to start.

Tap any game for what happened to the traveling team. Switch to all 56 games to see how small the disruption looks against a full Saturday, and how much of it landed in the late afternoon.

The 4 p.m. problem

Friday afternoon is when college teams fly. Most charters leave the day before a game, after a morning walkthrough, so a stop that began just before 4 p.m. Eastern caught the travel day at its peak. The FAA blamed a temporary telecommunications outage and lifted the stop about four hours later, but by then the night was gone for teams that had to cross the country.

Washington had the longest trip, about 1,854 miles from Seattle to East Lansing, and the team hadn't boarded when departures stopped. Busing was ruled out, so the kickoff at Michigan State slid from 4:15 to 5:30 p.m. ET. Arizona State, headed 1,844 miles to Orlando, got off its plane, went home and planned to fly Saturday morning. Its game at UCF moved from 3:30 to 5:30 p.m. ET and from ESPN2 to ESPNU.

The biggest push on Saturday itself went to Auburn. Its flight to Starkville was delayed, so the team got on buses for the 200-mile ride, and the noon kickoff at Mississippi State became 4:15 p.m. ET. FAU at Tulsa moved back 3 hours and 45 minutes, to 7:15 p.m. Baylor, one of four Big 12 teams still waiting to fly out Friday, now plays at No. 10 BYU at 7 p.m. ET instead of 3:30.

A late night in Tucson, and one game moved up

No. 2 Utah at No. 23 Arizona moved from 7:30 to 10:30 p.m. ET, which is 8:30 p.m. in Tucson. Fox kept its lead crew, Gus Johnson, Joel Klatt and Jenny Taft, on the game.

Kansas was the odd one out. Its charter landed in Pittsburgh at 3:50 p.m. ET Friday, four minutes before the stop, and the team was on the ground and on its way to Morgantown. Its game moved anyway, two hours earlier, from 5:30 to 3:30 p.m. ET, to take the ESPN2 window that Arizona State–UCF gave up.

Not every grounded team lost its kickoff. Purdue switched to buses for the 256-mile trip to Iowa City. North Texas rode to San Antonio. Alabama drove to Nashville. All three games stayed where they were on the schedule. Tennessee and Texas were also stuck on the ground Friday, and their kickoff times didn't change either.

Sunday in Norman

Ole Miss had nowhere to go. Its charter to Oklahoma couldn't leave Friday, and the players were sent home. The SEC moved the game to Sunday at 1 p.m. ET and told Oklahoma it would forfeit if it refused to play.

Oklahoma objected. Athletic director Roger Denny said the decision "isn't fair to our athletes, our institution, or to Sooner Nation." Oklahoma loses its home Saturday, and both teams lose a day of rest before next week's games.

What the chart doesn't show

A new kickoff time is only the part of the disruption that can be counted. Teams that flew Saturday morning or rode buses for hours arrive with less sleep and less time to prepare, and there's no column for that. The mileage in the map is a straight line from campus to campus, so it understates the bus rides. And the schedule could still change before kickoff.
