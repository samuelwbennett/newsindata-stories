Most coverage of Pew Research Center’s new projections has focused on one milestone: around 2035, Muslims are expected to outnumber Christians worldwide. A bigger change is happening inside Christianity itself. In 2020, about 31% of the world’s 2.3 billion Christians lived in sub-Saharan Africa. By 2070, Pew projects, 54% will, and Europe’s share will be cut in half, from 22% to 11%.

The map shows each country’s largest religious group in 2020 and in 2070. Christians remain the majority in 110 countries in 2070, down from 120. The United States, Canada and Germany lose their Christian majorities, and France, the United Kingdom and South Korea become majority unaffiliated. Muslims gain one majority country, Ivory Coast.

Christianity moves south

The world’s Christian population is projected to grow from about 2.27 billion to 2.96 billion. Sub-Saharan Africa accounts for more than all of that growth: Christians there are projected to more than double, from about 697 million to 1.6 billion, while their numbers fall in Europe and North America. Europe is projected to lose about 180 million Christians as its population ages and more people leave religion. The median age of Christians in sub-Saharan Africa was 20 in 2020; in Europe it was 45.

A new No. 1

The United States had more Christians than any other country in 2020, about 217 million. By 2070 that is projected to fall to 174 million, the largest drop anywhere, while Congo’s Christian population roughly triples, from 92 million to 299 million. Ethiopia, Tanzania, Uganda and Angola climb into the top 10. No European country remains in it.

Births, not converts

The projections turn on age and fertility far more than on conversion. Muslim women had an average of 3.1 children in 2020-25, Christian women 2.5 and religiously unaffiliated women 1.3, below the replacement level of about 2.1. Muslims, with a median age of 24, are the youngest major group.

Switching between the two largest faiths roughly cancels out: Pew projects about 9.2 million Muslims will become Christian and 9 million Christians will become Muslim over the 50 years. Christianity’s net loss of 105 million to switching goes mostly to the unaffiliated, who gain 117 million that way. Even so, the unaffiliated share of the world is projected to shrink, from 24% to 17%, because they are older and have fewer children. China alone is projected to have 361 million fewer unaffiliated people.

In the United States, Christians are projected to fall from 64% of the population to 48% by 2070, while the unaffiliated rise from 30% to 41%. Use the map above to find your own country.

How we made this: All figures are from Pew Research Center’s The Future of World Religions: Population Growth Projections, 2020-2070, published Nov. 18, 2026, and its country table. Pew modeled seven groups in 201 countries and territories using fertility, age, religious switching and migration, with researchers at the Krakow University of Economics; projections are not predictions and assume current trends continue. On the map, each country takes the color of its largest group, which can be a plurality rather than a majority; country shares are rounded to whole percents. Regional totals in the dot chart are rounded to the nearest 10 million. The map uses Natural Earth outlines.
