The NFL’s Thanksgiving starts on Wednesday night this year. The Packers play the Rams on Netflix in the league’s first Thanksgiving Eve game, followed by three games on Thursday and one more on Black Friday afternoon. That is five games and about 16 hours of football in three days.

The holiday slate has not always grown. In the league’s first decade Thanksgiving was crowded: in 1926, eight games involved NFL teams. As the league shrank and consolidated, so did the holiday schedule. It disappeared during World War II, and from 1953 to 1959 it came down to one game a year, in Detroit.

Dallas began hosting in 1966, and for four decades the holiday held two NFL games, with games from the rival AFL alongside them until the 1970 merger. The league added a night game in 2006, a Black Friday game in 2023 and the Wednesday game this year.

Why the NFL keeps adding games

The audience keeps growing. Last year’s Chiefs-Cowboys game drew 57.2 million viewers, the most for any regular-season NFL game, and Thursday’s three games averaged 44.7 million, up from 34.5 million in 2024, according to the league. It was the fourth straight year of record Thanksgiving viewership.
