The Lions host the Bears on Thursday in their 87th Thanksgiving game. Detroit has played on the holiday every year since 1945, and in five seasons before World War II, starting in 1934, its first year in Detroit. No team has played more Thanksgiving games, and no team has lost more: through last year the Lions were 38-46-2.

The best stretch came in the 1950s, when Detroit went 8-2 and played Green Bay every Thanksgiving from 1951 to 1963. In 1962 the Lions beat the Packers 26-14, Green Bay’s only loss on the way to a 13-1 season. The worst came from 2004 to 2012: nine straight losses, including a 47-10 loss to Tennessee in 2008, the year Detroit went 0-16.

Why Detroit keeps the game

That 0-16 season brought calls to replace the Lions with a more attractive matchup. The league kept them, and the calls faded as Detroit’s teams improved in the 2020s. Its Thanksgiving record has not followed: the Lions have lost eight of their last nine holiday games, and the one win was 23-20 over Chicago in 2024.

Dallas, the other permanent host since 1966, has done better on the holiday, with a 35-22-1 record.
