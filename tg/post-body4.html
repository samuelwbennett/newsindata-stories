An NFL game has 60 minutes on the clock. On Thanksgiving it takes a little more than three hours to play one, and the league plays three in a row. If Thursday’s games run like those of the past nine Thanksgivings, the first kickoff at 1 p.m. Eastern and the last snap at about 11:20 p.m. will be more than 10 hours apart, and a game will be in progress for about nine and a half of them.

On Thanksgiving, each minute of game clock takes about 2 minutes and 50 seconds of real time, about the same as in other regular-season games. The slowest minutes come at the end of each quarter, when the broadcast goes to commercials, and in the last two minutes of the first half, which take 10 to 13 minutes once the two-minute warning and timeouts are added.

Longer halftimes

The afternoon games run longer than usual mostly because of halftime. Over the past nine Thanksgivings, halftime of the Cowboys game averaged about 21 minutes and halftime of the Lions game about 20, compared with about 14 minutes for a typical regular-season game. The night game’s halftime is close to normal.

How we measured: We used the wall-clock time of every play in nflverse play-by-play data for Thanksgiving Day games from 2017 to 2025, leaving out overtime games and games with missing or faulty timestamps (8 Lions, 7 Cowboys and 8 night games remain). For each minute of game clock we measured the time from the first snap of that minute to the first snap of the next, then averaged by time slot. Real time runs from the opening kickoff to the last snap, so it leaves out the pregame show, the final play and the postgame. We placed each slot’s average at this year’s kickoff times; actual games will vary. Halftime is the time from the last snap of the second quarter to the first snap of the third. The comparison covers all regular-season games from 2017 to 2025. The dinner window is from a 2024 Pew Research Center survey.
