The San Francisco 49ers will play a home game on Sunday about 1,900 miles from their stadium. Their game against the Minnesota Vikings at Estadio Azteca in Mexico City is the NFL’s ninth regular-season game outside the United States this season, a record, in seven countries. Two months ago the 49ers were the visiting team in Melbourne, Australia, making them the first team to play in two foreign cities in one season.

The first regular-season game abroad was in 2005, when the Arizona Cardinals beat the 49ers 31-14 at the same stadium. The league started its London series two years later, played six Buffalo Bills home games in Toronto, and has since added Germany, Brazil, Ireland, Spain, France and Australia. This season brought the first games in Melbourne, Rio de Janeiro and Paris. Commissioner Roger Goodell has set a goal of 16 games abroad a year.

What the home team gives up

Each game abroad needs a designated home team that gives up a game in its own stadium. League rules require every team to do so at least once every eight years. So far 27 of the 32 have, in 71 games played abroad plus Sunday’s. The Baltimore Ravens, Denver Broncos, Houston Texans, New York Giants and Seattle Seahawks never have.

The Jacksonville Jaguars have given up the most, 14, all in London. They are the only team that runs its own games abroad and keeps the revenue, and team president Mark Lamping has told the BBC that a London game is financially better for the team than one in Jacksonville.

What a home game is worth

For most teams the trade costs money. The 49ers took in $176 million in net gate receipts in 2024, the most in the league, according to Sportico, which cited the NFL’s internal gate report. That was $40 million more than the Dallas Cowboys. The Athletic reported that giving up one home game at Levi’s Stadium costs the 49ers roughly $15 million to $20 million in general and club-seat ticket sales, plus suite, parking and concession revenue. The team also spent a sum in the low seven figures to train in Colorado Springs for Mexico City’s 7,000-foot altitude.

The league used to pay designated home teams a stipend to offset the lost revenue. It ended that payment in 2022 and now covers travel, according to Sportico. Washington Commanders owner Josh Harris, whose team gave up a home game in London in October, summed up the economics: “It’s not good.”

The league comes out ahead. The sports business publication SportsPro estimates the NFL values each game abroad at $55 million in media rights. The 49ers have their own reasons to go: they rank first among NFL teams in merchandise sales in Mexico and run the league’s largest Spanish-language digital channel.
