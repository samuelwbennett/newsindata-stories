On Wednesday the Federal Communications Commission approved a SpaceX plan to launch up to 1 million satellites carrying computer chips, a network of orbiting data centers the company calls Starmind. There are 17,566 working satellites in orbit today, according to the CelesTrak satellite catalog, and 11,192 of them are SpaceX’s own Starlink satellites. Since Sputnik in 1957, about 28,450 satellites have been launched in all.

It is the first FCC approval for a data center constellation. Each satellite would carry computing hardware drawing about 175 kilowatts on average, cooled by radiators and powered by solar arrays based on Starlink’s design, and the satellites would talk to each other and to Starlink by laser. The FCC set deadlines: at least one satellite in orbit by Nov. 25, 2033, 10% of the constellation by 2035, half by 2038 and the rest by 2040. SpaceX has said it plans to start launching in 2027.

Where they would go

The satellites would fly between 500 and 1,002 kilometers up. About 3,000 working satellites orbit in that band today; most Starlink satellites fly lower, below 500 kilometers. Filling the band with 1 million more would put about 330 times as many satellites there as there are now.

Amazon, which is building a Starlink competitor, the satellite company Viasat and the American Astronomical Society asked the FCC to deny the application, citing orbital debris, crowding and radio interference. The FCC called concerns about the effect of reentering satellites on the atmosphere speculative and said many questions about feasibility fall outside its mandate. It is reviewing other proposals, including an 88,000-satellite constellation from Starcloud and Blue Origin’s 51,600-satellite Project Sunrise.

The pace it would take

Last year rockets put a record 4,495 satellites into orbit, 3,169 of them Starlink. If SpaceX starts in 2027 and spreads launches evenly between the FCC’s deadlines, it would need about 11,000 satellites a year until 2035, about 133,000 a year until 2038 and 250,000 a year in the final two years.
