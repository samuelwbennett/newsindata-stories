About 46% of eligible Americans voted in the 2022 midterms, according to the University of Florida Election Lab, but the rate varied by more than 30 points between states. Oregon led at 62.3%, followed by Maine at 60.8% and Minnesota at 60.4%, while Tennessee was lowest at 31.0%, followed by Mississippi at 32.5% and West Virginia at 35.4%.

2018 was the high point

Midterm turnout reached 50.1% in 2018, the highest since 1914, and fell to 46.1% in 2022, according to the Election Lab's national series. The rate dropped in 42 of 50 states, by the most in North Dakota (58.7% to 41.7%), Tennessee (45.1% to 31.0%) and New Jersey (53.7% to 41.1%). It rose in eight states, by the most in Pennsylvania (51.8% to 53.9%), New Hampshire and Arizona.

Older voters are a bigger share than their population

Adults 65 and older made up 30.4% of 2022 midterm voters but 23.8% of voting-age citizens, according to the Census Bureau's survey of voters, while adults 18 to 29 made up 11.7% of voters and 19.6% of voting-age citizens. Half of 2022 voters (49.8%) cast ballots early or by mail, 10 points more than in 2018, according to the same survey.

The long view

Midterm turnout fell below 40% in most midterms from 1974 through 2014, and 2014's 36.7% was the lowest since 1942. The 2018 and 2022 rates were the two highest midterm figures in more than 50 years.

Early voting in 2026 is running ahead of 2022

More than 41.2 million Americans had voted early in person or by mail by the morning of Oct. 29, according to the Election Lab's early-vote tracker, equal to 37% of the 111.5 million ballots counted in all of 2022, with five days of voting left. Michael McDonald, who runs the tracker, said Oct. 26 that early voting was ahead of its 2022 pace in every state he had analyzed except Pennsylvania and Tennessee, and that it is "very possible" turnout will top 2018's rate. In Texas, 4.5 million people voted in the first week of early voting, 23.4% of registered voters, ahead of 2022's pace but just behind 2018's 24.3%, according to the Texas Tribune. Early votes show who has voted, not how they voted, and some of the increase may be voters casting ballots earlier rather than more people voting overall.

What's next

States report final turnout in the weeks after Nov. 3, and the Election Lab usually publishes its state estimates by early the following year. The Census Bureau's survey of who voted in 2026 typically arrives about 18 months after the election.
