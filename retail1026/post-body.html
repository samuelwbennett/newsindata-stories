Americans spent $748.2 billion at stores and restaurants in October, 6.8% more than a year earlier, according to the Census Bureau’s advance estimate released Nov. 17. Gas stations took in 30.3% more, more than four times the overall rate. Grocery stores grew 0.9%.

The figures aren’t adjusted for inflation, and higher pump prices explain most of the jump at gas stations. Regular gas averaged $4.45 a gallon in October, up from $3.24 a year earlier, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Prices climbed about $1 a gallon in March after the war with Iran began Feb. 28, and they have stayed above $4 since April.

The price rose faster than sales did, 37.5% versus 30.3%. That gap suggests stations sold somewhat less fuel than a year earlier. The comparison is rough, though: gas station sales also include snacks, drinks and other goods, and the price is a national average.

A third of the growth

Gas stations made up 9% of October sales but 33% of the $47.8 billion increase from a year earlier. Without them, retail and food services sales rose 4.9%, a little faster than inflation: the consumer price index rose 3.7% in the 12 months through September.

Online and other nonstore retailers grew 11.8% and supplied 28% of the increase. Restaurants and bars rose 7.0%, and electronics and appliance stores 7.7%.

The slow end

Grocery stores grew 0.9%, health and personal care stores 1.0% and car dealers 0.6%. Department stores took in 1.6% less than a year earlier. All four grew more slowly than overall prices, which rose 3.7%.

An early estimate

October’s numbers come from a smaller advance sample and will be revised. Total sales rose 0.2% from September, a change Census says is not statistically different from zero. September itself was revised up to $746.6 billion.

How we counted: Sales figures are from the U.S. Census Bureau’s Advance Monthly Retail Trade report for October 2026, released Nov. 17, 2026, Tables 1 and 2. They are seasonally adjusted and adjusted for holiday and trading-day differences, but not for price changes, and include only businesses with paid employees. Store types are the Census kinds of business; “online and other nonstore” is nonstore retailers (NAICS 454), and department stores are a subset of general merchandise. Shares of the increase use the change in seasonally adjusted dollar sales from October 2025. Gas prices are the BLS average price of unleaded regular gasoline (U.S. city average), via the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis; the inflation figure is the consumer price index for all urban consumers, not seasonally adjusted, September 2025 to September 2026.
