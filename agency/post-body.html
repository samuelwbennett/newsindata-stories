Seventy-three percent of U.S. adults have a favorable view of the National Park Service, the highest of 12 federal agencies and departments in a Pew Research Center survey taken Sept. 28 to Oct. 4 and published Oct. 26. Immigration and Customs Enforcement ranked last, viewed favorably by 35% and unfavorably by 56%. The National Weather Service (72%) and the U.S. Postal Service (68%) ranked second and third.

The top three draw both parties

Republicans and Democrats rate the Park Service, the Weather Service and the Postal Service within five points of each other. The Park Service is viewed favorably by 76% of Republicans and 74% of Democrats, and the Postal Service by 66% of Republicans and 71% of Democrats. The IRS gets the same rating from both, 38%, though that is among the lowest scores in the survey.

The parks behind the top rating

The land the Park Service manages is older than the agency. Congress set aside Yellowstone as the first national park in 1872, and President Woodrow Wilson signed the law creating the Park Service on Aug. 25, 1916, directing it to keep the parks "unimpaired for the enjoyment of future generations." The system now includes 63 national parks among more than 430 sites, with at least one in every state.

The parks recorded 323 million recreation visits in 2025, close to one for every U.S. resident and 2.7% below the record set in 2024, and 26 parks set their own records. Great Smoky Mountains, on the Tennessee–North Carolina line, drew 11.5 million visits, more than twice the 5.0 million at second-place Zion, followed by Yellowstone at 4.8 million and the Grand Canyon at 4.4 million. The Blue Ridge Parkway, which the Park Service also runs, drew 16.5 million, the most of any of its sites.

That reach crosses party lines. The Park Service is viewed favorably by 76% of Republicans and 74% of Democrats, a 2-point gap, the narrowest of any agency in the survey except the IRS, which both parties rate at 38%.

Enforcement agencies split the parties most

ICE has the widest gap of any agency, with 67% of Republicans and 9% of Democrats viewing it favorably, a 58-point difference. The Department of Homeland Security, which includes ICE, is seen favorably by 70% of Republicans and 22% of Democrats, and the Justice Department by 58% and 27%. Democrats rate four agencies more favorably than Republicans by 5 points or more: the CDC (54% to 41%), the Federal Reserve (53% to 46%), the Weather Service and the Postal Service.

Most agencies slipped from a year earlier

Nine of the 12 agencies had lower favorable ratings than a year earlier. The CDC fell the most, from 53% to 47%, and ICE fell from 40% to 35% while its unfavorable share rose from 49% to 56%. The Justice Department and the IRS each rose 1 point, and the FBI was unchanged at 48%.

What the data can't tell us

The survey measures opinions of each agency as a whole, not of specific policies or officials, and it doesn't ask why people hold those views. Pew interviewed 5,062 adults on its online American Trends Panel; the fact sheet does not list a margin of error, and changes of a few points from a year earlier may be within sampling error.
