Grand Theft Auto VI set two internet traffic records before anyone could play it. On Nov. 10, the day Rockstar Games let buyers start downloading the game early, traffic across DE-CIX’s internet exchanges worldwide hit a record 33.01 terabits per second. On the evening of Nov. 18, hours before the game unlocked at midnight in the United States, it hit 33.03 terabits.

DE-CIX runs internet exchanges, the buildings where networks such as internet providers, streaming services and cloud companies hand traffic to one another. Its Frankfurt exchange is the largest in Europe, with more than 1,100 networks connected. Frankfurt set its own record of 22.30 terabits on Nov. 10, about 13 complete downloads of the game every second, according to DE-CIX. Traffic around the launch ran 25% above a typical week at the start of the year. The game also set records at DE-CIX’s exchanges in Dallas, Chicago, Phoenix and Mexico.

The record it broke was barely a month old. On Oct. 13, 20 minutes after kickoff of a night of Champions League matches, DE-CIX traffic hit 32.10 terabits worldwide and 21.47 in Frankfurt. Soccer has set many of Frankfurt’s records, including the 2022 World Cup semifinal between France and Morocco.

Why a game moves that much data

The game is a download of about 210 gigabytes, roughly the size of 45 two-hour movies streamed in HD. Rockstar says it needs up to 212 gigabytes of storage. GTA VI has shipped more than 17 million digital copies, DE-CIX said, and the analytics firm Sensor Tower estimated $1.65 billion in digital preorders.

Other times one event moved the internet

Games have done this before. When Fortnite’s fifth season launched in July 2018, it pushed traffic on the content delivery network Akamai past 37 terabits per second, the most for any single game on its network at the time. In 2022, Akamai said a major sporting event and a big video game download drove its network to a record 250 terabits.

Live video is the other big draw. On Jan. 20, 2009, Barack Obama’s inauguration pushed Akamai to more than 7 million simultaneous streams, then a record. The biggest live streams are now about 10 times that size. India’s T20 World Cup final in March drew 73 million concurrent viewers on JioHotstar, and Jake Paul’s fight with Mike Tyson reached 65 million concurrent streams on Netflix in 2024. The GTA VI launch drew a peak of about 7.9 million people watching livestreams of the game across Twitch, YouTube and Kick, according to the analytics firm Streams Charts.
